# Learn-Me Bootstrap Interview

Complete this progressively with `learn-me`. Short answers are sufficient. The AI should not ask every question at once and should skip questions already answered by the materials.

## Phase 1: Goal and scope

1. What should this second brain mainly produce for you?
   - Blog articles
   - LinkedIn posts
   - Facebook posts
   - Discord messages
   - Other outputs

2. What should it do besides writing?

3. Should it normally think and write as you by default?

4. Are there subjects or situations where it must not speak as you?

5. Which parts should bootstrap first?
   - Core identity and principles
   - General writing voice
   - Platform-specific style
   - Language behaviour
   - Perspectives or roles
   - Topic-specific methodology

## Phase 2: Available materials

6. Which source collections are available?
   - Published articles
   - LinkedIn posts
   - Facebook posts
   - Discord messages
   - AI conversations
   - Original drafts and final edits
   - Rejected AI drafts
   - Personal notes
   - Work writing
   - Other

7. Where are these materials located?

8. Which materials were substantially written by you?

9. Which were AI-assisted but reviewed and approved by you?

10. Which contain your edits to AI-generated drafts?

11. Which rejected examples best show what you dislike?

12. Which collections are most representative of you now?

13. Which are outdated, experimental, private, or atypical?

14. Are any materials restricted to style learning only, with their facts or opinions excluded?

## Phase 3: Source classification

For each source collection, confirm:

15. Platform or publishing context?

16. Language and regional style?

17. Intended audience?

18. Typical length?

19. Perspective used, if different from your default self?

20. Main topics?

21. Was it published, approved privately, still a draft, or rejected?

22. How representative is it: high, medium, low, or negative?

23. Are there matching original and revised versions?

24. What should the AI learn or not learn from this collection?

## Phase 4: Default writing behaviour

25. If platform is omitted, what output type should be assumed?

26. If language is omitted, should output follow the language of your request, source material, or another default?

27. For Chinese, do you normally prefer Traditional Chinese, Simplified Chinese, Cantonese-influenced wording, standard written Chinese, or context-dependent usage?

28. When should technical and product terms remain in English?

29. What does `Mixed` mean for you?

30. If length is omitted, what is the normal length?

31. Should outputs normally use first person and speak directly as you?

32. What must the AI do when your approved opinion is not established?

## Phase 5: Platform behaviour

For each platform you use, answer only what differs from your normal voice.

33. Blog: expected depth, structure, personal reasoning, and typical length?

34. LinkedIn: desired professionalism, personal tone, hook, formatting, hashtags, and closing style?

35. Facebook: desired intimacy, storytelling, humour, informality, and typical length?

36. Discord: expected brevity, shorthand, technical density, humour, and conversational style?

37. Are there platform behaviours you dislike, such as clickbait hooks, forced questions, excessive emojis, hashtags, or calls to action?

## Phase 6: Perspectives and audiences

38. Which perspectives genuinely represent you?
   - AI/data engineer
   - Researcher
   - Career professional or mentor
   - Parent
   - Investor
   - Technology enthusiast
   - Personal commentator
   - Other

39. For each perspective, what experience or knowledge supports it?

40. How should that perspective change emphasis without changing your core identity?

41. Which audiences materially change your explanation style?
   - Children
   - General public
   - Engineers
   - Colleagues
   - Executives
   - Parents
   - Investors
   - Other

42. Are there topics where the AI should explore a possible view rather than present it as your established position?

## Phase 7: Validation

After analysing the materials, `learn-me` should present findings and ask:

43. Which inferred patterns feel accurate?

44. Which are caused by platform rather than personality?

45. Which are caused by language rather than platform?

46. Which are outdated or over-generalised?

47. Which apparent contradictions are actually context-dependent?

48. Which proposed rules should remain suggestions rather than approved identity?

49. Is any source too private or sensitive to retain in the repository?

50. May `learn-me` create the initial `learn/*` branch and pull request?
